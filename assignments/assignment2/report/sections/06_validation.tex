\section{Validation Approach}\label{sec:validation}
Validation has two parts. End-to-end tests measure the accuracy of the calibrated kinematics over the work volume against the hidden ground truth (Steps~9--10). Unit tests check each component in isolation, mostly on synthetic data with a known answer. Hidden simulator values are used only to score completed estimates.

\subsection{End-to-end accuracy}
For each evaluated configuration the error is $\Delta F=\Frame(I+\sk(\vct{\alpha}),\vct{\epsilon})$ with $\norm{\vct{\alpha}}$ the angle of $\log\Delta R$ (\fn{metrics.frame_error}). The forward error is Eq.~\eqref{eq:alg-fwd-metric} on the 729 poses of the template's grid, $\theta_{1,3}\in\{100,300,500\}$\,mm, $\theta_2\in\{-200,0,200\}$\,mm, $\theta_{4,5}\in\{-1.3,0,1.3\}$ and $\theta_6\in\{-2,0,2\}$\,rad. The inverse error compares 40 targets drawn from this grid with the poses reached after $\Fun{MoveJoints}$, so it includes the repeatability of the final move. Both are reported as RMS and maximum over the poses and compared with the handout's targets, $10^{-3}$\,rad and $0.2$\,mm, applied to the maximum. The complete pipeline is repeated for five robot and tracker seeds and for both plans. The handout's definition relative to the robot base, $(F^*_{\mathrm{ee}}(\vct{\theta}))^{-1}\Fun{ImprovedFwdKins}(\vct{\theta})$, is evaluated in separate runs on random configurations over the full joint ranges (Section~\ref{sec:res-base}).

\subsection{Unit tests}
\fn{tests/test_simulation.py} (23 tests, supplied) checks the simulator: the error bounds, homing, motion time, joint limits, nominal kinematics, viewing volume, clock and calibration-body rules. \fn{tests/test_student.py} checks the student modules. Tables~\ref{tab:unit-supplied} and \ref{tab:unit-own} list its 23 cases; synthetic data are generated by our own model with known parameters of the handout's size, so the correct answer is exact.

All 46 tests pass (\texttt{python -m pytest tests -q}, about $10$\,s). The two tests that use simulated noise, marker refinement and data collection, have thresholds several standard deviations wide and passed in repeated runs.

\subsection{Limitations}
The end-to-end results cover one scene design and a finite set of seeds, and the tracker's reading noise is not seeded, so repeated runs of one seed differ slightly. The template's grid covers the wrist joints to $\pm1.3$ and $\pm2$\,rad rather than their full ranges; the full ranges are covered by the separate runs of Section~\ref{sec:res-base}. The rotation $c$ about $\vct{d}_1$ cannot be observed, so poses relative to the robot base are known only to within its admissible interval. The unit tests verify the components against our own model, not against the simulator's hidden error model.
